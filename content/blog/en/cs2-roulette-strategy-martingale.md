Search for "CS2 roulette strategy" and you will find the same systems again and again: Martingale, reverse Martingale, Fibonacci. They promise steady wins. Here is what they actually do, with simple maths.

## How the Martingale system works

You bet on a colour that pays 2x. After every loss you **double** the bet; after a win you go back to the starting bet. The idea: one win covers all previous losses plus one unit of profit.

It sounds safe — until you look at a losing streak. Starting with 1 coin, after 8 losses in a row you have already lost 1 + 2 + 4 + … + 128 = **255 coins**, and your next bet must be 256 coins. All of that to win back **1 coin**.

## How often do long losing streaks happen?

Take a colour with a 47% chance to win (the exact number depends on the site's wheel). The chance of losing 8 times in a row is about 1 in 160 — it feels rare. But over a session of 200 spins, the chance of hitting **at least one** 8-loss streak somewhere is roughly **45%**. Play a few sessions and it becomes almost certain. One bad streak wipes out hundreds of small wins.

## The real problem: the house edge

Every spin has a negative expected value. With a 47% chance at 2x, each 1-coin bet returns on average 0.94 coins — you lose about 6% per bet in the long run. A betting system only changes **how** you lose (many small wins, then one big loss), not **whether** you lose. Fibonacci and other progressions work the same way, just slower.

- **Set a loss limit and a time limit**, and stop when you reach either.
- **Never raise bets to win back losses** — that is the Martingale trap in disguise.
