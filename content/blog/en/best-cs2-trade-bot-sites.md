A trade bot site lets you swap CS2 skins instantly: you put your items on one side, pick items from the site's inventory on the other, and the trade goes through in a few seconds through Steam. No waiting for a buyer, no haggling in Steam groups.

The catch is the price. Bot sites rarely charge a visible fee — instead they earn on the **spread**, the difference between what they pay for your skin and what they ask for theirs. So the "best" bot site is the one where that difference is smallest for the skins you actually trade.

## CS.MONEY — three modes in one account

CS.MONEY has been running since 2016 and is the only site of the three that combines a trade bot with a peer-to-peer market. That matters: if the bot price for your skin looks low, you can list it on the Market instead and usually get more. Buyers pay no fee on the Market.

Before cashing out, keep in mind: identity verification (KYC) is required before the first withdrawal, money from Market sales is held for 7 days, and card withdrawals go mainly to Visa.

## Tradeit.gg — bot trading for CS2 and Rust

Tradeit focuses on bot trading and supports both CS2 and Rust items. It earns on the spread rather than a fixed fee, so compare the price it offers for your skin with a P2P market before trading. In 2025 it took over the skin rental service LootBear.

## SkinSwap — fast swaps and instant sell

SkinSwap also works with CS2 and Rust and adds an instant sell option on top of bot trading. Like Tradeit, it uses a spread instead of a visible commission.

## How to pick the right one

- **You want the best price when selling** — a P2P market (CS.MONEY Market, Waxpeer, DMarket) almost always beats a bot.
- **You want a quick upgrade or downgrade** — any of the three bots works; check the same skin on two sites and pick the better ratio.
- **You trade Rust items too** — Tradeit or SkinSwap.

## Stay safe

Only trade on the official domains, confirm every trade in the Steam mobile app and check the bot's name in the confirmation. Never share your Steam API key — scammers use it to swap trade offers. More tips in our guide on [avoiding CS2 scams](/en/guides/avoid-cs2-scams/).
