How to avoid CS2 trading scams

Most CS2 skin scams work the same way: they trick you into logging in on a fake page or giving away access to your trades. Learn the common tricks and follow these rules.

The most common scams

  • Fake login pages: a site that looks like Steam asks you to sign in and steals your account.
  • API key scam: a scammer gets your Steam Web API key and cancels or replaces your trade offers with fake ones.
  • Impersonation: someone copies a friend's or a trader's name and avatar and asks you to send items.
  • Fake middlemen: “I'll hold the items for both of you” — and then disappears.
  • Too good to be true: an offer far above market price is almost always a trap.

10 rules to stay safe

  • Log in only on the official Steam page — check the address bar every time.
  • Turn on Steam Guard Mobile Authenticator.
  • Never share your Steam Web API key. If you are unsure, revoke it on the official Steam API key page.
  • Always confirm trades in the Steam mobile app and check the items and the partner there.
  • Never send items first to someone you do not know.
  • Do not use “middlemen” from Discord or chats.
  • Do not click links from strangers in Steam chat or Discord.
  • Trade only on well-known sites with a long history and real reviews.
  • Read withdrawal rules before you deposit anything.
  • If an offer feels rushed or too good — stop and check.
